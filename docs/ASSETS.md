# Authored world assets

The release uses original parametric mesh recipes and deterministic PBR texture generation. The source is the asset: Vite packages these local TypeScript modules into the application, which generates its mesh buffers and texture pixels without asset downloads. No paid assets, unlicensed model libraries, external texture URLs, or generated preview images are used. Creator: ContractorKeith; copyright: Keith Bloemendaal; license: MIT.

The machine-readable [asset manifest](../public/assets/manifest.json) records source, creator, license, modification, redistribution notice and exact local path for every asset family. Include [the original-asset license](../public/assets/licenses/ORIGINAL-ASSETS-MIT.txt) and the installed Three.js MIT notice when redistributing the build. Three.js is a rendering/geometry dependency, not the source of the wagon, animal, person or terrain designs.

## Pipeline and art direction

The visual direction is grounded stylized realism: weathered oak, pale bent-wood hoops, woven warm canvas, dark leather, worn iron and muted vegetation. Meshes use `MeshStandardMaterial`, metallic/roughness response, sRGB albedo and non-color bump data. The generated sky environment provides filtered PBR reflections. The runtime should use WebGL 2, sRGB output, ACES filmic tone mapping and a shadow-enabled renderer.

- `geometry.ts`: continuous cross-section lofts, tapered beams, rounded joinery, swept curves and static material batching. Hero body surfaces are continuous lofts, with smaller anatomical or construction details added explicitly. Loft faces have an automated outward-normal check.
- `materials.ts`: deterministic 256×256 wood grain/knots, canvas weave, bark, hide, leather, earth and stone maps. These are authored algorithms rather than photographs, scraped textures or AI image assets. Fine texture scale and material roughness distinguish the surfaces at close range.
- `wagon.ts`: modeled bed/interior, ten floorboards, sideboards/stakes, tailgate hardware, driver bench/step/handholds, axles/springs, bent hoops, sagging canvas shell/seams/ties, iron-tired spoke wheels, cargo and tools. Wheels rotate from signed distance, not elapsed time.
- `animals.ts`: continuous ox and deer body/neck/head meshes, horn/antler anatomy, muzzle/eyes/ears, split hooves, tails, articulated legs with ground-adjusted foot contact, and a shaped rabbit with speed-driven hopping. Two ox coats and a connected yoke/harness give the team readable depth from the seat.
- `people.ts`: clothing silhouettes, bent limbs, faces, hands, footwear, hat/scarf/pockets/buttons, idle head movement, usable tent interior and furnished trader stall. The provisions sign uses original stroke-letter geometry.
- `terrain.ts`: actual height variation, carved water beds, trail ruts, grass/reeds, irregular broadleaf crowns, pine boughs, outcrops, stream banks and distinct regional horizons. Trees, stones and vegetation use instancing; grass uses seven separately culled patches. Ground uses 48m chunks.
- `atmosphere.ts`: animated PBR water normals and shoreline foam, procedural sky/clouds/sun/stars, soft fire/smoke sprites, embers, flickering point light, rain/snow, ground snow tint and distance fog. Shelter removes nearby precipitation under the canopy; rain reduces exposed fire strength.

The preserved [HTML study](pioneer-trail-first-person-demo.html) is a visual/interaction reference only. It remains untouched and is not an application asset. Actual integrated browser screenshots, gameplay and performance evidence belong in the acceptance evidence set; generated source or a passing compile alone does not establish finished visual quality.

## Runtime contract

Import `createWorld` and its exported types from `src/game/world/index.ts`. Call `createWorld(scene, { terrain, river, seed, quality })`; quality is `low`, `balanced` or `high`. Campaign names `Plains`, `Forest`, `RiverValley`, `Hills`, `Mountains` and `Desert` map to appropriate regional recipes. Each region is 240 playable meters long with scenery margins. Region creation is synchronous and deterministic; visual seed never touches the campaign RNG.

Coordinates use meters, Y up and +Z forward. The runtime owns the returned `wagon` group transform, movement/physics and camera. Its seated eye is `seatLocal = (0, 2.35, 0.5)`. The wagon bed spans approximately x ±1.2 and z −2.4…1.9; runtime body collider center z −0.45, half-width 1.25 and half-length 2.3. The oxen centers are x ±0.79, z 5.03; the full train reaches z 6.66. Wheel-center height/radius is 0.78m. Set the group ground height and gentle pitch/roll from the actual terrain.

`heightAt(x,z)` interpolates exactly the visible mesh triangles, including trail ruts and river beds. `waterHeightAt(x,z)` exposes water level for a floating wagon and fishing bobber. `obstacles` contains stable IDs and world-axis box bounds for tree trunks, large rocks, furniture, people and river hazards. Leaves/high branches, grass and tiny stones have no blocking colliders. Collision uses conservative core footprints for irregular rocks and tent bedding, rather than invisible full-canopy boxes.

`locations` exposes real camp, trader, companion, supplies, fishing-water and (when applicable) riverbank positions. Initial camp is left of the trail at z≈22; the trader is right at z≈29. A crossing region has `river = { startZ:104, endZ:136, current:0.8, depth:1.2 }`, a dry bank interaction at z92, and four visible `river-rock-N` hazards with navigable gaps. The runtime owns costs, outcomes and current forces.

`wildlife` entries expose `{ id, animal:'Deer'|'Rabbit', root, position, alive, harvested }`; `position` is `root.position`. Descendants have `userData.wildlifeId` for raycast attribution. Paths are deterministic functions of the supplied world time. The runtime raycasts only live targets, stores the hit position before marking an animal dead, restores saved positions before the next update, and marks `harvested` only after collection. Dead animals remain as settled carcasses; collection hides them. The world never grants rewards or mutates campaign data.

Call `update({ dt, time, speed, distance, cameraPosition, sheltered, weather, daylight })` each active frame. `distance` is signed actual travel in meters; `time` is visual simulation time, frozen while paused. Active activities persist their elapsed time; ordinary region ambience starts again after reload. `weather` is `clear`, `rain`, `snow` or `fog`; `daylight` is 0…1. For the same time and wagon pose, living wildlife positions are deterministic. Patrol envelopes keep full live bodies in existing dry clearings with a westward escape corridor; gait follows actual displacement. Saved carcass and collected-target positions remain fixed. `shelteredAt(x,y,z)` checks the physical tent and trader roof volumes; the runtime also checks the wagon canopy. `dispose()` is idempotent, releases mesh/instance/material/texture/shadow resources, removes the region and restores the prior scene fog/environment. The runtime owns renderer disposal and listeners.

## Verification

`npx vitest run src/game/world/world.test.ts` checks visible-ground raycasts against collision height, actual river depth/hazards/bank location, outward hero normals, atmospheric shell clipping/uniforms, regional relief, wheel/distance coupling, restored carcass/collection behavior and scene resource disposal. The focused suite contains fourteen tests, including exact trunk overlap, clear patrol corridors, displacement-driven avoidance gait, and identical physical layouts across graphics presets. `npx tsc -p tsconfig.app.json --pretty false` and `npx oxlint src/game/world` verify the integration surface and static checks.

Final screenshot comparison, hardware performance and long-run transition stability must be measured in the integrated game. These recipes intentionally use approximate vegetation, particle smoke/flames and controllable articulated animals instead of expensive fluid, fur or harness simulation.
